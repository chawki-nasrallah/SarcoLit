# `v0.1-corpus` — sarcopenia + EMG abstract corpus

**Date:** in progress (started 2026-05-02)

## Scope

PubMed E-utilities ingestion of sarcopenia and EMG-of-aging-muscle abstracts into a deduplicated, versioned dataset on disk (HuggingFace `datasets`-compatible). This is the input substrate for the `v0.2` baseline RAG. No retrieval, no generation, no eval at this tag.

## Locked search strategy

Decided iteratively in PubMed's web UI before any code was written. The choice was driven by precision (a noisy corpus poisons every later milestone) over recall.

```
(
    (
        "Sarcopenia/diagnosis"[Majr]
        OR "Sarcopenia/classification"[Majr]
        OR "Sarcopenia/physiopathology"[Majr]
        OR "Sarcopenia/pathology"[Majr]
    )
    OR
    (
        ("Muscular Atrophy/diagnosis"[Majr] OR "Muscular Atrophy/physiopathology"[Majr])
        AND ("Aging"[MeSH] OR "Aged"[MeSH])
    )
    OR
    (
        "Electromyography"[MeSH]
        AND (
            "Sarcopenia"[MeSH]
            OR "Muscular Atrophy"[MeSH]
            OR "Muscle Weakness"[MeSH]
            OR "Aging"[Majr]
        )
    )
)
AND humans[MeSH]
AND english[la]
AND hasabstract
AND 2000:2026[dp]
NOT ("Mice"[MeSH] OR "Rats"[MeSH] OR "Disease Models, Animal"[MeSH])
```

**Result counts at lock time (web UI, 2026-05-02):**
- Total corpus: **6,800** papers.
- EMG-bearing subset (`Electromyography[MeSH]` or EMG mentioned in title/abstract): **1,322** papers (~19% of corpus) — confirms EMG is well-represented, not just nominally present via the EMG branch.

### Decision rationale

#### Date floor: 2000

The modern sarcopenia consensus is post-2010 (EWGSOP 2010, EWGSOP2 2019), but EMG-and-aging research has substantive pre-2010 roots. A 2000 floor preserves foundational EMG-of-elderly-muscle work without going so far back that definitional drift in sarcopenia papers becomes unmanageable. Pre-2000 literature excluded as a deliberate trade-off.

#### Sarcopenia branch: `[Majr]` + subheadings, not `[MeSH]` alone

- `[Majr]` (MeSH **Major Topic**) requires sarcopenia to be a *central* topic of the paper, not merely mentioned. Eliminates noise like microbiota reviews where sarcopenia appears in passing as one of several age-related conditions.
- Subheadings (`/diagnosis`, `/classification`, `/physiopathology`, `/pathology`) restrict to papers about *what sarcopenia is and how to detect it* — the agent's intended scope.
- Subheadings deliberately *not* included: `/etiology` (catches "X causes sarcopenia" comorbidity papers), `/complications` (catches "sarcopenia → other outcomes"), `/therapy`, `/drug therapy`, `/rehabilitation` (treatment scope, deferred to a later milestone), `/epidemiology` (prevalence studies are mostly observational, dilute precision).
- Trade-off: we lose review papers that are about sarcopenia but lack subheadings. Acceptable for `v0.1` precision; revisit in `v0.4` if the eval shows missing definitional content.

#### Muscular atrophy branch: same discipline, paired with aging

`Muscular Atrophy[Majr]/diagnosis` or `/physiopathology`, paired with `Aging[MeSH] OR Aged[MeSH]`. Catches muscle-aging papers that aren't tagged with sarcopenia directly (the term came late to the literature; older papers may use "muscular atrophy in aging" framing instead).

#### EMG branch: `[MeSH]`, not `[Majr]`, with strict partner clause

- An initial `Electromyography[Majr]` formulation gave only 179 EMG-bearing papers in the corpus (3.1% share) — too thin for a project where EMG is the domain differentiator. Most relevant EMG papers don't have EMG as their *central* topic; they use EMG as one of several muscle-assessment tools. `[MeSH]` captures those.
- Partner clause kept strict — EMG must co-occur with `Sarcopenia`, `Muscular Atrophy`, `Muscle Weakness`, or `Aging[Majr]`. Without it, `Electromyography[MeSH]` would pull in motor-neuron, post-stroke, peripheral-nerve, and pelvic-floor EMG papers that share the technique but not the topic.
- Free-text branch (`"HD-sEMG"[tiab]`, `"surface EMG"[tiab]`) was held in reserve as a further loosening lever; not needed once `[Majr]` → `[MeSH]` brought EMG share to ~19%.

#### Global filters

- `humans[MeSH]` — required, but **soft** filter only. PubMed records can carry both `Humans[MeSH]` and `Animals[MeSH]` tags simultaneously (e.g., a mouse-models review that motivates work by referencing human disease). This is necessary but not sufficient for human-only.
- `english[la]` — language uniformity. Multilingual deferred.
- `hasabstract` — records without abstracts can't be embedded; useless to us.
- `2000:2026[dp]` — date range as above.

#### Animal exclusion: explicit `NOT` clause

Because `humans[MeSH]` is soft, an explicit `NOT ("Mice"[MeSH] OR "Rats"[MeSH] OR "Disease Models, Animal"[MeSH])` is needed to actually keep the corpus human-clinical. The `Disease Models, Animal[MeSH]` umbrella catches animal-modeling papers across species we didn't list (rabbit, primate, pig). Comparative papers that cite animal data only in the discussion remain — they aren't tagged with these animal-subject terms.

## Licensing

PubMed **abstracts** are redistributable under the [NLM Terms and Conditions](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/databases/download/terms_and_conditions.html). This corpus contains abstracts only — no full text. Retained metadata per record: PMID, title, abstract, journal, publication year, authors, MeSH terms, DOI.

## Scope decisions deferred

### Abstracts only for `v0.1`; full-text upgrade staged for `v0.4`

Considered ingesting full text for the PMC Open Access Subset alongside abstracts. Deferred for three reasons:

1. **License surface.** "Open access" is not uniform — only the PMC OA Subset has explicit redistribution rights. Mixing redistributable abstracts with restricted-license full text would require a per-paper licensing audit, work better spent once measurement justifies it.
2. **Engineering surface.** Full text adds a separate PMC pipeline (JATS/NXML parsing, table/figure handling, ~30× more text per record) and forces a real chunking-strategy decision before we have the eval harness (`v0.3`) that would let us tune chunking responsibly.
3. **Better story for the project.** With an abstract-only baseline at `v0.1`/`v0.2` and an eval harness at `v0.3`, adding full text at `v0.4` becomes a *measured* upgrade — quantifiable lift in retrieval and generation metrics, not "more text". That comparison is the artefact.

Roughly 20–35% of the 6,800 papers are expected to be in the PMC OA Subset; the remainder will remain abstract-only even after the `v0.4` upgrade.

### Chunking moved from `v0.1` to `v0.2`

The original checklist placed chunking in `v0.1`. Moved to `v0.2-rag-baseline` because chunk size is a *retrieval* parameter: its only sensible test is whether it improves what the retriever returns, and there is no retriever at this tag. Chunking blind here would bake in an untested guess. Deferring keeps `v0.1` to a clean, inspectable corpus and lets `v0.2` choose chunking against real retrieval behaviour (Chip Huyen Ch. 5 read alongside that work). Abstracts are short (~1–2k chars), so single-abstract-as-one-chunk is a viable baseline to measure against anyway.

### Implementation notes

- **Parser uses stdlib `xml.etree.ElementTree`, not `lxml`.** `lxml` was pre-pinned in Step 2, but ElementTree handled the PubMed XML without issue, so the heavier dependency wasn't needed. (Left in `pyproject.toml` for now; candidate for removal.)
- **Output is JSONL, not HF Arrow.** Chosen to avoid pulling in `datasets` just to *write* the corpus; JSONL stays `datasets`-compatible for v0.2's *read* path while remaining human-readable and git-diffable. `datasets` likewise still pinned but unused at this tag.
- **Structured abstracts flattened.** Multi-section abstracts (`<AbstractText Label="METHODS">` …) are concatenated into one plain-text paragraph; section labels dropped.
